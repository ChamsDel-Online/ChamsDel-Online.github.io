IkigaiNiche turns one: same problem, four different people.

By Chaminda DelpagodageNovember 25, 2026Staying or pivoting

One year ago tomorrow, on November 26, 2025, I made the first commit on the project that is now IkigaiNiche. Since then it has been renamed three times and built for four different people. Four of my products have postmortems on this site. IkigaiNiche is not one of them.

This week I went back through the commit history and wrote down every change that mattered. Here is the log, what I see in it now, and how to write the same log for your own project tomorrow.

The log, from my own commits

  • November 26, 2025. First version, called JanFifteenth: a community for working parents starting their founder journey, with an ikigai finder and a waitlist.
  • November 28, 2025. Two days later. The commit message, word for word: "Pivoting away from working parents, to helping people find their ikigai niche." The ikigai finder became the product, with AI helping fill in the four circles.
  • December 2, 2025. Renamed to ikigainiche.app.
  • February 28, 2026. Rebranded as Ikigai Niche Finder, with a PDF report, pitched as profitable niche discovery.
  • April 16, 2026. Made it permanently free ("always free" replaced "free for a limited time") and added a "start from your job role" option so experienced professionals did not have to list their skills by hand. By then it was a five-step tool with AI suggestions, traffic light scoring, reflective tests and ranked results.
  • November 16, 2026. Rebuilt as a guided journey for someone who has never had a business idea.
  • November 19, 2026. Renamed IkigaiNiche, and rewrote the terms to match the free product it is now.

Why the last rebuild happened

This is how I put it on X on November 16:

My wife has to write a business plan for a class.

I told her: don't invent one for the grade. Find one you'd actually start.

Problem: she's never had a business idea and didn't know where to begin.

So I rebuilt Ikigai Niche Finder for exactly that person.

Reading the log back, that is the first version I built for one person I could name. Every earlier version was written for a group: working parents, people curious about their ikigai, people looking for a profitable niche, professionals with a job title.

Building for her changed what the product asks. It never asks for an idea, because she did not have one. It asks about you, one question per screen: what you lose hours to, what people come to you for, who you find yourself rooting for. Then a person you would enjoy helping for years, their problem in their own words, and an honest read on whether anyone pays to fix it. The rebuild also deleted most of what the older version did: skill cataloging, traffic light scoring, reflective tests, AI chat, the PDF download and the community features. Those were for the earlier people.

What I see in the log now

The problem never moved. Every version is for someone who wants to start something and does not know what. What kept moving was the person. Changing who it is for is a pivot too, even when the problem stays the same, and each one means the feedback you get next comes from somebody new.

Some changes were not answers. The first pivot came two days after the first commit. I cannot find anything in the history that says a user asked for it. That change came from me. It is the same pattern I wrote about in small bets only work if you stay: some exits are answers, and some happen before the idea had a chance to say anything.

A real person is easier to build for than a group. This morning I posted:

single most important skill for a new founder:

putting yourself in the shoes of your ICP.

two things can happen when you do that:

a) you see what's actually bothering your ICP.

b) you realize that's not your actual ICP.

either one is a big win.

With "working parents" I could only imagine their shoes. With one real person, I can ask her where she gets stuck.

I cannot tell you yet whether this version works. It is nine days old. What I can say is that it is the first version where I know exactly who to ask.

How to write your own pivot log tomorrow

  1. Print your history oldest first. In a git repo: git log --reverse --date=short --format="%ad %s". If you do not use git, your old landing pages, launch posts and pricing emails work too.
  2. Mark every change to four things: the name, the headline, the price, and who it is for.
  3. Write one line per mark: who it was for before, who it was for after, and what told you to change.
  4. Read the "what told you" column. If most lines are empty or say "me", you are changing on mood, not on anything a user did.
  5. If the person keeps changing and the problem does not, pick one real person you can name and build the next version for them. Ask them before you change it again.

One thing to do tomorrow

Before you change who your product is for, write down what the current person did with it: came back, asked for something, paid, or nothing. If you cannot fill that line in, you have not given them a fair run yet. The two questions I ask before I pick a problem help with the other half: whether the problem itself is worth staying with.

If you have never had a business idea

That is the person IkigaiNiche is built for now. I wrote the steps it walks you through, so you can do them with a notebook, in how to come up with a business idea when you have never had one. Or try IkigaiNiche itself: about 10 minutes, free, no sign-up. If you have a few people in mind and cannot pick one, how to choose a niche you will not abandon has four tests for it.
