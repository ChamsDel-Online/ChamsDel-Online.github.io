Frequently asked questions

How many hours do you need for a side project?

A realistic minimum is 8 to 12 focused builder hours per week to make visible progress on a side project. Below 6 hours per week, context-switching eats most of your sessions and momentum dies. Above 15 hours per week you can ship a small SaaS MVP in 6 to 10 weeks if the scope is honest.

How do I find time for a side project while working full time?

Pick four weeknights and one weekend block, and guard them like meetings. A 90-minute weeknight session plus a 3-to-4-hour weekend block gives you roughly 10 builder hours a week without burning out. The key is consistency across 6 to 12 weeks, not heroic 20-hour weekends that you can't repeat.

Is 5 hours a week enough for a side project?

Five hours a week is enough to maintain a simple project, not to launch one from scratch. At that pace, expect 4 to 6 months to ship a small MVP, and only if your scope is ruthlessly narrow. If you can't find more hours, shrink the product instead of stretching the timeline.

How do I estimate realistic builder hours as a solo founder?

Track one honest week before you plan. Count only sessions where you shipped code, copy, or a decision, not time spent reading Twitter or reorganizing Notion. That honest number, multiplied by your roadmap weeks, is your real time budget. Most solo founders overestimate by 40 to 60 percent.

What's the best weekly schedule for a side project?

Deep work on 3 to 4 weeknights after dinner for 60 to 90 minutes, plus one 3-hour weekend block on Saturday or Sunday morning. Keep the same slots every week so the habit compounds. This pattern produces around 7 to 10 builder hours weekly, which is enough to ship a focused MVP in a quarter.

How do I know if my side project timeline is realistic?

Take your honest weekly hours, multiply by the number of roadmap weeks, and compare that to the scope. If the scope needs 200 hours and you have 60, the timeline is fantasy, cut scope, don't extend time.
