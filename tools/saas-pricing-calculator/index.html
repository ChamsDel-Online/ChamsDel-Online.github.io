How to calculate SaaS spend

SaaS spend is the total you pay for software subscriptions over a period. For each tool, multiply the seats you pay for by the price per seat. If a tool is billed yearly, divide the yearly price per seat by 12 to get a monthly figure, so every tool is compared on the same basis.

  1. List every subscription. Card statements and invoices are the most reliable source, because tools get added by different people.
  2. For each one, note seats paid, price per seat, and whether the price is monthly or yearly.
  3. Monthly spend per tool = seats × monthly price per seat. Annual spend = monthly spend × 12.
  4. Add the tools together for total SaaS spend. Divide by headcount for SaaS cost per employee.

Flat-fee tools with no seats work too: enter 1 seat and the full price.

How to find SaaS savings

The calculator looks at two savings levers that you can check yourself, without guessing:

  • Unused seats. Seats paid minus seats actually used, times the price per seat. Admin panels usually show last login or last active dates, which is the honest way to count used seats.
  • Monthly to annual billing. If a vendor offers a lower price for paying yearly, the saving is the used seats times the monthly price times 12 times that discount. The calculator applies this only to tools you marked as billed monthly, and only after unused seats are removed, so the same dollar is never counted twice.

Annual billing is a commitment. It only saves money on tools you are sure you will keep for the year. If you are deciding which overlapping tools to cut, the Weighted Decision Matrix helps you compare them on the criteria that matter to you.

How to calculate SaaS pricing (what to charge)

The pricing calculator starts from the outcome you need, not from what other products charge. It adds your monthly income target and tool costs, then divides by (1 minus your buffer) to cover tax, refunds, failed payments and churn. That gives a monthly revenue target. Dividing the revenue target by your expected paying customers gives the recommended monthly price.

The number only holds if the niche can pay it. Before committing to a price, check the idea with the SaaS Idea Scorecard and the niche with the Niche Clarity Scorecard. If you are not sure what income target to type in, the Freedom Calculator works out what your life actually needs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate SaaS spend?

For each subscription, multiply the seats you pay for by the price per seat. If a tool is billed yearly, divide the yearly price per seat by 12 first. Add all tools together for total monthly SaaS spend, and multiply by 12 for annual spend. The spend calculator on this page does this for you.

How do I calculate SaaS cost per employee?

Divide total SaaS spend by your headcount. Enter your headcount in the spend calculator and it shows SaaS cost per employee per month and per year.

How do I calculate SaaS savings?

Check two things. Unused seats: seats paid minus seats actually used, times the price per seat, times 12 for the yearly saving. Annual billing: for tools billed monthly, used seats times the monthly price times 12 times the annual discount the vendor offers. The calculator removes unused seats first, so savings are not counted twice.

Is annual SaaS billing cheaper than monthly?

Only when the vendor offers a lower price for paying yearly, and only for tools you will keep all year. Look up each vendor's actual annual price, enter the discount in the calculator, and compare it with the flexibility you give up.

How do I calculate what to charge for my SaaS?

Add your monthly income target and your monthly tool costs, divide by (1 minus a buffer percentage for tax, refunds and churn) to get a monthly revenue target, then divide that by the number of paying customers you realistically expect. The pricing calculator on this page runs this formula.

How many paying customers do I need to make $3,000 MRR?

Divide the revenue goal by the price. At $30 per month you need 100 paying customers, at $50 per month you need 60, and at $99 per month you need 31. A higher price needs fewer customers but is a harder sale.

Does this calculator save or send my data?

No. Both calculators run entirely in your browser with plain JavaScript. Nothing you type is sent to a server or stored, and the page never asks for passwords or payment details.
