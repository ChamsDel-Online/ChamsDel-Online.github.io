How the quiz works

Your secondary archetype adds texture and range. Most memorable creators on X have one dominant archetype and one adjacent one they weave in to stay interesting.

Frequently asked questions

What is a brand archetype quiz?

A brand archetype quiz is a short assessment that maps your answers to one of the 12 Jungian archetypes — Innocent, Sage, Explorer, Rebel, Hero, Magician, Everyman, Lover, Jester, Caregiver, Creator, or Ruler. The result tells you the personality your brand should embody across voice, visuals, and content.

How accurate is this brand archetype quiz?

The quiz uses 10 questions, each mapping to a single archetype. That's enough to reveal your dominant and secondary archetypes with strong confidence. It won't capture every nuance of a decades-old brand, but for indie creators and founders it gives a directionally correct starting point in under three minutes.

What are the 12 brand archetypes?

Innocent, Sage, Explorer, Rebel, Hero, Magician, Everyman, Lover, Jester, Caregiver, Creator, and Ruler. Each represents a distinct motivation — from safety and wisdom to freedom, liberation, mastery, and control — and translates to a measurably different brand voice.

Can my brand be more than one archetype?

Yes. Most strong brands have one dominant archetype (70–80% of the voice) and one secondary archetype that adds range. Mixing more than two tends to produce a forgettable brand because no single personality stays in mind.

How do I use my archetype on X or Twitter?

Your archetype controls sentence length, vocabulary, stance, and emotional tone. A Sage teaches in frameworks; a Jester subverts expectations in under 20 words; a Rebel names what everyone else is pretending not to see. Write every reply and post in your archetype's voice so your brand compounds instead of drifting. ReplyWisely keeps you on-voice at scale.

What's the difference between this and a personality quiz?

A personality quiz tells you who you are privately. A brand archetype quiz tells you who your brand is publicly — the persona your audience should experience consistently. They can overlap, but they don't have to.
